# Building a Campaign Setting - A Game Master's Action List

**Purpose:** the concrete actions a GM should take to turn a pile of inspiration into a setting that actually plays well at the table. Ordered roughly the way a campaign gets built. Each action maps to a Campaign Workshop pillar (`/gm`) and to vault entity `type`s, so what you plan here has a home in the Library.

> Rule of thumb: a setting is ready to run not when it's *finished*, but when it has **one place, a handful of people who want conflicting things, and a reason the party can't sit still.** Everything below deepens that core - start it, don't perfect it.

---

## 0. Before anything - set the frame

These are decisions, not entities. Make them first; they constrain every choice after.

- [ ] **Pitch in one sentence.** "Frontier town slowly being bought out by a death cult that pays in gold." If you can't say it in a line, the players won't feel it.
- [ ] **Tone & genre.** Grim survival? Heroic high fantasy? Political intrigue? Pick one primary, one secondary.
- [ ] **Scale.** One valley, one city, one continent? Smaller is almost always better to start.
- [ ] **The central tension.** What is *wrong* in this world right now that play will be about? A setting with no pressure is a museum, not a campaign.
- [ ] **Player buy-in.** What kind of characters does this setting invite? Tell players before session zero.

---

## 1. World & Places - build the stage

*Pillar: World & Places · types: `location` · `city` · `village` · `dungeon`*

The party needs a place to stand before they need a plot.

- [ ] **Anchor location.** One home base the party returns to (town, ship, keep). Name it, give it a mood, three notable spots, one problem.
- [ ] **3–5 nearby places** the party can reach in a session: a dungeon, a wilderness, a rival settlement. Each needs: a reason to go, something there, a danger.
- [ ] **A map** - even a rough one. Distances create decisions ("we can't make it back before dark").
- [ ] **Sensory hooks per place.** One smell, one sound, one sight. This is what makes a room memorable, not its dimensions.
- [ ] **Connect places to people.** Every location links to at least one NPC or faction. An empty room is set dressing; an occupied one is a scene.

**Vital info for a location:** name · what it's *for* in the story · who controls it · what the party can do there · one secret or hidden thing · how it connects to other places.

---

## 2. Characters & NPCs - build the cast

*Pillar: Characters & NPCs · types: `npc` · `character`*

NPCs are the campaign. Plots are just what NPCs do to each other.

- [ ] **A quest-giver / patron** - someone who points the party at trouble.
- [ ] **An antagonist** with a goal, not just stats. The villain should *win* if the party does nothing.
- [ ] **2–3 neutral locals** the party will actually talk to - innkeeper, fence, rival adventurer.
- [ ] **Give each one a WANT.** What they want from the party (or the world) is the engine of every scene.
- [ ] **Give the important ones a SECRET.** Something the party can discover that changes how they see the NPC.
- [ ] **One distinctive trait each** - a verbal tic, a scar, a habit. Play it consistently; it's how players remember "the limping notary."
- [ ] **Relationships between NPCs.** Who hates whom, who owes whom. Conflict the party can step into.

**Vital info for an NPC:** name · role/occupation · **want** (motivation/goal) · **secret** · personality in one line · appearance hook · voice/mannerism · faction & location ties · what they offer the party (hook, info, threat).

---

## 3. Factions & Powers - build the conflict

*Pillar: Factions & Powers · types: `faction` · `organization` · `religion`*

Factions turn a static map into a pressure cooker. They act whether or not the party is watching.

- [ ] **2–4 factions** with clashing goals. Overlap creates friction; friction creates plot.
- [ ] **Each faction needs:** a goal, a method, a leader (an NPC), a stronghold (a location), and a resource it controls.
- [ ] **A faction clock.** What does each faction *do* over the next few weeks if uninterrupted? Advance it between sessions. This makes the world feel alive.
- [ ] **Where the party fits.** Which faction courts them? Which one they threaten? Give them a side to take - or a reason to refuse.
- [ ] **A religion or belief system** if it matters - gives villains conviction and gives players moral terrain.

**Vital info for a faction:** name · goal · method · leader (NPC link) · seat of power (location link) · resource/leverage · who it's at war with · how it advances when ignored.

---

## 4. Quests & Story - build the plot

*Pillar: Quests & Story · types: `quest` · `event` · `timeline`*

Don't write a plot the party must follow. Write situations they must respond to.

- [ ] **An opening hook** that drops the party into trouble in the first 15 minutes. Don't make them go looking for the adventure.
- [ ] **3+ quest threads** of different shapes: a clear job (go kill/fetch), a mystery (something doesn't add up), a moral choice (no clean answer).
- [ ] **Stakes the party cares about.** "Save the world" is abstract. "Save the innkeeper who comped your rooms" lands.
- [ ] **A ticking clock.** A deadline, an advancing threat, a thing that gets worse. Urgency is the difference between a quest and a chore.
- [ ] **Branches, not rails.** For each quest, plan at least two ways it can go. Assume players ignore your favorite plan.
- [ ] **A campaign timeline** of events that happen *regardless* of the party - so the world has momentum.

**Vital info for a quest:** name · the hook (how they hear of it) · who wants it done (NPC) · where it happens (location) · the obstacle · the stakes · the clock · the reward · what happens if ignored.

---

## 5. Bestiary - build the challenges

*Pillar: Bestiary · types: `creature` · `monster` · `encounter`*

Combat is a language for expressing the setting's dangers. Make fights say something.

- [ ] **A signature threat** for the region - the thing that says "you are *here*." (Sahuagin on the coast, ghouls in the plague city.)
- [ ] **Encounters with a reason to exist.** Why is this monster here, now? Guarding what? Hunting whom?
- [ ] **Variety of pressure:** a brute fight, a swarm, a social/avoidable threat, a puzzle-creature. Not every problem is solved with damage.
- [ ] **Terrain in every fight.** Cover, hazards, verticality, escape routes. Flat empty rooms make flat fights.
- [ ] **Non-combat outs.** Can the encounter be talked past, sneaked around, bargained with? Players love a fight they *chose* over one they were forced into.

**Vital info for an encounter:** what & how many · where · why it's there · terrain/hazards · the goal (kill? delay? guard?) · the non-combat alternative · what it drops or reveals.

---

## 6. Treasures & Lore - build the depth

*Pillar: Treasures & Lore · types: `item` · `artifact` · `lore`*

Lore and loot are the reward for paying attention. Deliver them through play, not lectures.

- [ ] **Rewards that hook.** The best treasure creates the next adventure - a map, a key, a cursed blade someone wants back.
- [ ] **A few signature items / artifacts** tied to factions or history. An item with a story beats a +1 sword.
- [ ] **History the party can *discover*** - in ruins, in NPC mouths, in items. Reveal lore as a reward, never as a wall of text.
- [ ] **Mysteries with answers.** Plant questions early; know the answers yourself even if players never reach them.
- [ ] **Consistent internal logic.** Magic, gods, money - pick the rules and hold to them. Players trust a world that doesn't contradict itself.

**Vital info for an item:** name · what it does · where it came from · who wants it · the hook/story it carries · the price or catch.

---

## 7. Wire it together - the relationship web

A pile of entities is not a setting. The links between them are.

- [ ] **No orphans.** Every entity links to at least one other (vault rule). NPC → location + faction. Quest → NPC + location + reward. Faction → leader + seat + rival.
- [ ] **Triangles, not lists.** Three NPCs who each want something from the other two = infinite scenes.
- [ ] **One thread the party can pull** from session one that touches every pillar within a few sessions.

---

## 8. Before session one - readiness check

You're ready to run when you can answer **yes** to:

- [ ] Can I describe the **opening scene** and the trouble in it?
- [ ] Is there **one place** detailed enough to improvise in?
- [ ] Are there **3+ NPCs** with wants the party will collide with?
- [ ] Is at least **one faction** actively making things worse?
- [ ] Is there a **clock** - something that gets worse if the party stalls?
- [ ] Do I have **one clear hook** and at least **two threads** if they ignore it?
- [ ] Can the world **move without the players** - do I know what happens if they do nothing?

If yes to all: run it. The rest you build *between* sessions, in response to what the players actually chase.
